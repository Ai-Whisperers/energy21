# 🏆 The Startup Masterguide: Best Practices of the 1%

*A compilation of the exact setups and philosophies used by YCombinator, Sequoia Capital, and the world's most successful tech companies.*

If you want to build a company that scales seamlessly, avoids co-founder lawsuits, and raises money quickly, you must lay a foundation made of concrete, not sand. Here are the universal best practices.

---

## 1. Corporate & Legal Setup (The "Boring" Stuff that Saves Companies)

**Incorporate Correctly from Day 1**
- **The Gold Standard:** If raising VC money, the world expects a **Delaware C-Corp**. Even if you are based in Paraguay or localizing through an Estonian OÜ for European taxes, your overarching "HoldCo" for investors is almost always Delaware or Cayman.
- **Why?** Delaware corporate law is predictable. Investors know exactly how shareholder rights work there. They will not invest in an obscure LLC where they have to hire local lawyers to understand their protections.

**The "Cliff" and Vesting (Do Not Skip This)**
- **The Rule:** All founders and key early employees must be on a **4-year vesting schedule with a 1-year cliff**.
- **The Reality:** Without it, if a co-founder leaves 3 months in due to a disagreement, they could walk away with 30-50% of your company. Dead equity renders a startup un-fundable. The 1-year cliff ensures anyone who quits early leaves with nothing.

**Clean Intellectual Property (IP) Assignment**
- **The Rule:** The company, NOT the individuals, must own the code, trademarks, and algorithms.
- **The Execution:** Every single person who writes a line of code or draws a logo for Solstein must sign a Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (CIIAA) before they start work. 

**Accounting:** 
- Separate business and personal expenses immediately. Never buy personal items on the corporate card. It creates "piercing the corporate veil" liability, meaning your personal assets could be seized if the company gets sued.

## 2. Operations & Metrics

**The "Default Alive" Philosophy**
- Paul Graham (YCombinator) coined this: A startup is "default alive" if it can reach profitability with the cash it currently has, without needing to raise another round. 
- *Pro Tip:* Keep your personal living expenses incredibly low while validating the product. Burn rate is the fastest killer of great ideas.

**What to Track (The KPIs)**
The best companies don't track vanity metrics (like LinkedIn followers or website visits). They track:
1. **MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue):** The lifeblood of SaaS.
2. **Gross Margin:** Revenue minus cost of goods sold (COGS). AI companies often struggle here due to massive LLM API costs. Keep API costs strictly monitored per user. If your margin is below 60%, investors will view you as a low-value service business, not software.
3. **NDR (Net Dollar Retention):** Are your existing customers spending more money with you over time, or churning? 120%+ NDR is world-class.
4. **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) Payback:** How many months of MRR does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring that customer? >12 months is dangerous.

## 3. Product & Go-To-Market

**"Make Something People Want"**
- Better to have 100 users who *love* your product than 10,000 who *kinda like* it. Build for a hyper-niche first (e.g., European Energy PE funds). Dominate that niche completely, then expand.

**Do Things That Don't Scale (Initially)**
- In the beginning, don't build a fully automated, self-serve onboarding portal. Do the onboarding manually via Zoom. Talk to the clients. See where they get confused. Only automate once you perfect the manual process.

**Charge More Than You Think You Should**
- Startups chronically underprice their products. 
- Price acts as a signal of quality. If Solstein costs €2K/year, PE firms will think it's a toy. At €150K/year, they think it's an alternative to McKinsey. **Charge for the value you create, not the time it takes you to run the code.**

## 4. The Data Room (Due Diligence Preparation)

When a VC or PE firm says "We are interested," they will ask for your Data Room. You should have this ready *today*. It usually exists in DocSend or a highly organized Google Drive.

**Folders every Data Room must have:**
📁 **1. Corporate Documents** (Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, Cap Table export from Carta).
📁 **2. Financials** (P&L for the last 2 years, Balance Sheet, Projections model).
📁 **3. Legal & IP** (Signed IP agreements from all founders/contractors, Trademark registrations, DPAs for GDPR).
📁 **4. Key Contracts** (The signed €60K pilot agreements, the Energy21 SLA, vendor agreements >$10k).
📁 **5. HR** (Standard employment contracts, vesting schedules).

*If you hand an investor a perfectly organized data room within 24 hours of them asking, it signals extreme competence and increases valuation leverage.*
